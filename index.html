01 / Current projects

Understand safety.
Make it stronger.

We use interpretability to understand why AI safeguards fail and test how to improve them. Three connected projects focus on open-weight models and tool-using agents.

01 / EVALUATE

Find hidden vulnerabilities.

Can a model’s internal representations reveal safety weaknesses that its answers miss?

Our approach
Test internal safety signals against behavioral evaluations, then check whether they predict failures on unseen attacks.
Where we are
Building on our prior representation-level evaluation research.
Next step
Validate the metrics across model families and develop a reproducible audit protocol.
Read the foundational paper ↗
02 / STEER

Help agents stay on task.

When can an internal intervention prevent an unsafe action and help an agent recover?

Our approach
Compare steering with text-based monitoring, action blocking, and replanning in a controlled tool-use environment.
Where we are
Early experiments are exploring intervention timing; improved overall task completion is still an open question.
Next step
Build a steering prototype and benchmark actual tool actions, recovery, and safe completion of authorized tasks.
Agent safety & control
03 / STRENGTHEN

Make safeguards harder to undo.

Can safety training withstand later weight modifications while preserving useful adaptation?

Our approach
Rehearse safeguard-removal attacks during training and compare defenses under matched compute budgets.
Where we are
A small, controlled pilot motivates further testing; it does not yet establish general tamper resistance.
Next step
Replicate on larger models and unseen attacks, measuring safety durability alongside capabilities and legitimate adaptation.
Tamper-resistant post-training

These projects are in development. We aim to share reproducible methods and useful findings, including when an approach does not work.
